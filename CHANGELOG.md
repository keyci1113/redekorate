# Changelog

All notable changes to redekorate are documented here. The project does not publish versioned releases yet; entries are grouped by development phase, newest first.

## Unreleased

- **Daily brief.** A sun button in the header, next to the bell, opens a panel on the right with the day at a glance: *My day* (my open tickets that are overdue, due today or due later this calendar week, at most 8, with a link to My work), *What's new* since I last opened it (new responses per study, new insights, tickets from the feedback widget, tickets assigned to me and comments that @mention me), *Live studies* with total and today's responses, and *Upcoming milestones* due within 7 days with their progress. Empty sections are hidden, and a calm *Nothing due today* shows when there is nothing at all. From 1024px up the panel pushes the page instead of covering it; below that it is a sheet over the page. It stays open across pages, is remembered per browser, closes with X, Escape or the sun button, and refreshes on open and with *Refresh*. A dot on the sun shows that something is new since the last open. `GET /api/brief` (a few aggregate queries over every project the person can open, following each project's sections), `GET /api/brief/status` and `POST /api/brief/seen`. "What's new" starts at the last open (`users.brief_seen_at`, never more than 7 days back; the last 24 hours on a first open), and "today" is the viewer's time zone. Migration `028_brief_seen.sql`. Mentions in ticket comments now carry `comment: true` in their notification, which tells them apart from being tagged in the fields.
- **REST API at `/api/v1`.** A plain REST API for scripts and integrations: 36 operations that read (`GET`), create (`POST`) and edit (`PATCH`) projects, milestones, tickets, studies and their results, insights, documents and the earlier versions of studies, documents and ticket descriptions. No `DELETE`, and nothing about accounts, admin, sharing or members. Reference in `docs/api.md`.
  - **Access.** Bearer tokens only (personal access tokens and OAuth access tokens); the session cookie never opens it. A read-only token gets `403` on anything but `GET`.
  - **Strict edits.** A `PATCH` changes only what it sends; an unknown field or query parameter is a `400` naming it; dates must be days that exist.
  - **Guarded content.** Replacing study blocks or settings, document data or a ticket's description saves a version, refuses to empty it and takes `base_updated_at` (`409` with the current `updated_at` when it changed). Blocks, diagrams and document sections go through the same content checks as the AI apps' edits.
  - **Outside people's data stays in.** Results come without participant records, recordings or contact details typed into answers; tickets without widget reporters' details (a summary stays, `from_feedback_widget` marks their text); no share or roadmap tokens. Widget screenshots are their own editors-only call (`getTicketScreenshot`).
  - **One shape for errors.** English JSON `{ error, issues? }`; a known path with another method is a `405` with `Allow`. The OpenAPI 3.1 document at `/api/v1/openapi.json` is generated from the same operation list the server runs.
  - **Limits.** `/api/v1`, `/api/v1/tools` and `/mcp` share one allowance per account (240 calls a minute).
- **Services layer.** The logic for projects, studies, insights, documents, tickets and milestones, the roadmap and My work moved out of the route handlers into `server/src/services/*`: functions that take an actor and plain input. The web routes are now thin wrappers, and the REST API calls the same functions in process. Route tests written before the move (`routes/pinned.db.test.ts`) pin what the web app receives, and still pass. `docs/architecture.md` describes the layers.
- **MCP: tickets and milestones.** New tools `list_tickets` (filtered by status, column name, label, type, priority, assignee or title text, and paged), `get_ticket`, `create_ticket` (with `insight_ids`), `update_ticket` (`base_updated_at` for the description), `comment_on_ticket` (internal only), `link_ticket`, `get_ticket_screenshot` (editors and owners), `list_milestones`, `create_milestone`, `update_milestone` and `my_work`. `list_versions` and `restore_version` also take a ticket. Ticket results carry `from_feedback_widget` and an `untrusted_content` notice when the text came from the feedback widget; `update_ticket` and `update_milestone` are marked `destructiveHint`; the server's instructions name the new tools. Reference in `docs/mcp.md`.
- **Connect AI apps: REST guide.** A third address card (*REST API*) and a *Scripts and other apps (REST)* guide with `curl` examples against `/api/v1`. The old scripts guide for the AI operations is now *Other AI apps (HTTP)*. The error table lists `405`.

- **Project start page.** Creating a project now opens `/projects/:id/start`, which asks what to set up first: a user study (the method chooser, as before), the ticket board or project management (milestones). The order and a one-line hint follow the project's type (research and design start with a study, software, AI, automation and IT with the board). It creates nothing itself, and *Decide later* goes straight to the project.
- **Dashboards.** Three dashboards across projects at `/dashboards` (Studies, Tickets and Projects), reached from one *Dashboards* control on Home and on every dashboard, with project and period filters kept in the URL. Stat tiles with the change against the previous week or period, a column chart (responses per day, tickets created against closed per week), bar lists (methods, status, workload, source, type, severity, widget pages), studies that need attention, the live studies with Home's result views, overdue and due-soon tickets, a projects table with derived health and next milestone, upcoming milestones and recent activity. Chart colours are theme tokens checked for colour-blind separation and contrast in both themes; every bar has a hover and keyboard tooltip and the data is also a table for screen readers. Studies count only projects whose *UX research* section the person can open, tickets only those with *Project management*.
  - **Click into a figure.** Tiles, bars and columns are buttons that open a window with the items behind the number, each a link: overdue tickets, live studies, the tickets of one person, type, status, source, week or widget page, the studies of one method or day, the insights of one severity, milestones and one project's health. What the page already loaded is filtered in the browser (`web/src/dashboards/drill.ts`); the rest comes from `GET /api/dashboards/tickets/items` and `GET /api/dashboards/studies/items` (same filters and access rules, up to 100 items with the total). Home's figure windows now use the same modal and drill model (moved from `components/home/` to `web/src/dashboards/`), and Home shows the *My work* card.
- **Share a document with people by email.** The *Share* dialog of every document, diagram and sitemap now has *People with access*: invite someone by email as *Can view*, *Can comment* or *Can edit* on that document only. Someone with an account gets access at once (and an email when SMTP is set up) and finds it under *Shared with me* on the Projects page; someone without one gets a 7-day invitation that creates the account, skips the welcome wizard and opens the document. Project editors change or remove each person and cancel pending invitations, and a person can take themselves off. The server enforces it: a share never opens the project, its other documents, studies or project comments; it never deletes, duplicates, copies (*Save as…*) or re-shares the document; a person's role on the document is the higher of their project role (with the *UX research* section open) and the share. Comments on the document follow the same role. The read-only public link is unchanged. MCP tools go through the same routes, so `get_document` and `update_document` respect shares too. Migration `027_document_shares.sql` adds `document_shares` and document invitations.
- **Feedback widget.** A project can put a feedback button on other websites and apps with one tag (`/widget.js`, plain JavaScript in a shadow root, English and Spanish). Each message becomes a ticket labelled `feedback` with the reporter, page and browser details, custom data and attachments: a screenshot (attached at once, marking it up with Undo and *Clear marks* is optional, pasting an image attaches it), a screen recording of up to 2 minutes and files; attachments are optional. The settings page (*Tickets → Feedback widget*, `/projects/:id/widget`) picks the assignee and the ticket type per kind, what people can send, privacy and looks, with a live preview drawn by the same script, the install snippet, allowed domains, pause, key rotation and a test feedback button. Only the allowed domains can load the configuration or send; the public endpoints (`/api/widget/:key/config|feedback`) use no cookies, are rate limited (`WIDGET_PER_IP_10MIN`), drop what the project turned off and accept files by content under random names, through the upload security scan. `data-rk-private` areas are blacked out in screenshots and recordings. A user shows as verified only when the host signs the id with the widget secret. Editors can remove one attachment from a report (the bytes are deleted and the history says who removed what), and erasing the reporter's data now deletes the files too. Guide in `docs/widget.md`. Migration `026_feedback_widget.sql`.
- **Notifications.** People on a ticket hear when they are assigned or @mentioned, when it moves to another column and when someone comments; whoever makes the change is never told. A bell in the header shows the last 30 with an unread count (mark one or all as read), and each notice is also emailed once after the response unless the person turns emails off in *My account → Notifications*. Migration 025_notifications.
- **Milestones, roadmap and My work.** Milestones get start and due dates (migration 024_milestone_dates) and a *Milestones* tab with state (planned, on track, at risk, behind, done), progress and tickets by status. The *Roadmap* tab draws milestones and studies on a month or quarter timeline and can publish a revocable read-only link (`/rm/:token`) with no tickets, people or response counts. Organizations get a combined roadmap (`/orgs/:id/roadmap`). *My work* (`/my-work`, in the top navigation) lists your tickets across projects by due date. Project report links can include *Milestones and progress*.
- **Tickets.** Each project gets a *Tickets* tab under *Project management*: a board with drag and drop (cards reorder inside a column and move between columns, with an undo toast), a list with sorting, filters and bulk edit, a full-height side drawer and a full ticket page (`/projects/:id/tickets/:number`). Tickets have a per-project number, type, priority, assignees and tagged people, due date, milestone, labels, comments with @mentions (a mention tags the person), an activity history, links to insights, studies, documents and other tickets, merge into a similar ticket, and delete (owners). Each project can define its own board columns (2 to 12, each counting as To do, In progress, In review or Done); Done columns go last, the editor explains each status and rows drag by a handle. Migrations 021_tickets and 022_ticket_columns.
  - **Tickets and research.** *Create ticket* on an insight links both ways; *Create study* on a ticket opens the method chooser and links the new study to it (the results page shows its tickets). *Convert to insight* opens a dialog with title, body, severity (from the ticket's priority, urgent = critical) and tags; a ticket converts once, with relation `converted`.
  - **Content versions.** Migration 023 adds `ticket_id` to `content_versions`, so ticket descriptions can keep earlier versions like studies and documents.
- **Sections and per-project access.** A project has two sections, *UX research* and *Project management*. For each project collaborator and each organization member you choose *Everything*, *UX research* or *Project management*, and an organization member can reach every project or only the ones you pick, with a preview of the result before saving (`AccessEditor`). Tabs of a closed section are hidden and the API refuses them. Invitations carry the same choices. Migration 020_access_areas; existing people keep both sections and every project.
- **Project files.** A new *Files* tab in each project is a small shared drive for PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Markdown, text and CSV files (25 MB each by default, `FILES_MAX_MB`; migration `041_project_files.sql`). Everyone in the project can view and download them; editors upload, rename and delete. Uploads are checked against their type and go through the upload security scan; the bytes live under `UPLOAD_DIR/.files`, never on the public uploads route, and are removed with their project. The *Documents* tab is now *UX documents*.
- **Delete a response from the list.** Each participant row in *Results → Participants* has its own delete button (editors only), so a test or invalid response goes without opening the row first.
- **Session recordings.** A study can record each participant's session (off by default, *Design → Record the participant's session*): page changes, clicks, scrolling and screen changes with rrweb 2.2.0, not video, with typed text masked. The participant sees a notice before starting and an indicator throughout. Pieces are sent every few seconds, stored compressed (migration `040_session_recordings.sql`), capped per session and idempotent on retry. *Results → Participants* marks recorded sessions and replays them (play, seek, speed, skip pauses); editors can delete a recording, and recordings are deleted after 90 days (`RECORDING_RETENTION_DAYS`). rrweb loads only when a study records or a replay is opened.
- **More diagram starters and a card shape.** Four new starters in *New diagram*: app overview (lanes for outside systems, the main cycle in order and all-year work, with dashed data lines), process by role (swimlanes), AI integration (source systems, an AI agent, a high-stakes check with human review, results and a log of every action) and org chart. A new *card* shape (shortcut C) shows its first line as a bold title and the rest as details, with labels such as "Who:" in bold. A test keeps every starter free of overlapping shapes and inside its sections, and the dialog lists starters from the model so none can be left out. The card shape is back in the mood board toolbar too.
- **Time study: before and after.** A time study can now hold several observation rounds of the same people, buckets and tools. *Add an “After” round* on the Summary keeps everything logged so far as *Before*; the log and the summary then show one round at a time, rows can be moved between rounds, and a *Before and after* tab compares any two rounds on hours per person per week (so rounds of different lengths or sizes line up): days observed, hours per day, hours in the measured tools, and every activity and tool with the change in hours and percent, led by the biggest change. Shared links and Word, PDF, Markdown and CSV exports include the comparison, a summary per round and a round column in the log. Rounds are stored in the document (no migration); documents without rounds work as before.
- **Ranking drag.** Ranking questions use pointer-based drag and drop: the item follows the pointer and the others slide out of the way, on mouse and touch alike. The up and down buttons still work.
- **Dropdown arrows.** Every dropdown in the app now draws the same arrow with space around it, instead of the browser's own, which sat flush against the edge. It is one CSS rule for all selects, so new ones need no class.
- **Prototype tasks.** The mission instructions stay visible while the participant works, an HTML prototype really loads on the task's start screen (the tracker waits for the prototype to settle instead of a fixed delay and reports whether it got there), a task can start on the flow's first tap, the prototype's own instructions are shown, and *I'm done* / *I can't complete it* are bigger.
- **Study styling.** A study can set its own button color and text (automatic by contrast, light or dark); without one the buttons follow the brand color. The color pickers take a hex code or the browser pipette, and the study logo size is adjustable. Brand-tinted selections stay readable in dark mode.
- **Connect AI apps page.** A new `/connect` page (account menu, linked from *My account* and *Help*) sets up and explains MCP and the AI operations over HTTP: both addresses with copy buttons; translated setup guides for Claude, ChatGPT (connector and GPT actions), Claude Code, Cursor (user-wide file only, so a token never lands in a repository) and scripts, with the real addresses and a token just created filled in; access tokens (read and edit or read only) and connected apps; the tools an app gets, read from the server's registry; and the rules and error codes. *My account* keeps a short summary that links to it.

Same tokens, same rules, strict input, the same per-account rate limit as `/mcp`; writes are marked `x-openai-isConsequential` so a GPT confirms them first. Errors are JSON with the app's status (`400` with `issues`, `401`, `403`, `404`, `409`, `429`).

  - **Edits no longer wipe fields.** Editing an insight with only `{ reviewed: true }` reset its body, tags and evidence and set severity and confidence back to medium, and editing a participant's name cleared their email, phone, notes and consent: zod's `.partial()` keeps defaults. - **Security scan of uploaded files.**
  - **One step for every upload.** Images and HTML prototypes go through `server/src/lib/fileScan.ts` before their bytes are written.
  - **Built-in checks, always on.** The real type (magic bytes) must match, so a file with HTML markers but binary content behind them is refused. The same step refuses PDFs with active content (`/JavaScript`, `/JS`, `/Launch`, `/EmbeddedFile`, `/AA`, `/RichMedia`, `/XFA`, also hex-escaped or inside compressed object streams), Office files with macros and text files that are HTML/SVG pages with scripts, ready for any upload path that accepts those types. Prototypes are meant to run (sandboxed), so they get only the type check and the antivirus.
  - **Optional antivirus.** With `CLAMAV_HOST` set, every file is streamed to ClamAV (`clamd` INSTREAM over TCP, no new dependency). When clamd is configured but cannot answer, uploads are refused with a *try again* message; `CLAMAV_FAIL_OPEN=true` lets them through. The README has a `docker-compose.override.yml` snippet for a `clamav/clamav` service.
  - **Messages.** A refused file gets a `422` (or a `503` when the antivirus is down) with a translated message that says why; the reason is logged on the server. The web upload screens already show it.
- **No more blank pages when moving around the app.**
  - **What happened.** After a deploy, a tab opened before it still asked for the old build's page files. The server answered those with the app's HTML page instead of a 404, the browser refused it, and with nothing to catch the error React removed the whole app: a blank white page until a manual reload. Any other error while showing a page did the same.
  - **Now.** A page file that fails to load reloads the tab once, onto the new build. Any other error shows a short message with *Reload* and *Go to home*, inside the app frame so the menu still works, and clears when you move to another page.
  - **Loading.** While a page loads, only the page area shows the spinner; the top bar stays.
  - **Server.** A missing file under `/assets` is now a plain `404`.
- **Move and zoom your profile photo.** After uploading a profile photo (in the welcome steps or *My account*), a dialog lets you drag it inside the circle and zoom in with a slider, the mouse wheel or the keyboard (arrows, + and −); *Adjust* or clicking the photo reopens it later. The position is saved as a fragment on the photo's address (`/uploads/x.gif#c=x,y,zoom`), so there is no migration, nothing is re-encoded and animated GIFs keep moving. The server accepts only that exact shape, and a test guards it.
- **Maintenance screen.** During a deploy, open tabs show *We're updating redekorate* instead of failing requests, check `/api/health` every few seconds and reload by themselves once the app is back. A tab still on the old build also reloads when it hits a page whose files changed. Admins get a *Maintenance mode* switch under *Users*, with an optional message, for when they're working on the instance. While it's on, everyone else gets a 503 with `{ maintenance: true }` on the API and MCP, and the maintenance screen; signing in and the health check stay open, and admins keep working with a banner. Migration 016 adds `app_settings`, so the switch survives deploys. `deploy/maintenance.html` plus Caddy and Nginx snippets in the README cover the few seconds when the container itself is down.
- **Say when you don't have access.** Pages no longer just hide what a role can't do. A short note with a lock explains it: on a project (every tab), a study's results, a document, a diagram or sitemap, commenters read that they can view and comment but not edit, and read-only people that they can only view, with whom to ask for editor access (the project owner, by name when known). Collaborators tells non-owners that only the owner adds people or changes roles, and an organization's People tells members that admins invite and set permissions. The results page's insights tab also stops offering *New insight* to people who can't create one.
- **Formatted text in diagram shapes.** Double-clicking a shape opens a formatting bar: bold, italic, underline and strikethrough on the selected words (⌘B, ⌘I, ⌘U, ⇧⌘X), bulleted and numbered lists (⇧⌘8, ⇧⌘7), and for the whole text four sizes (a new extra large), left, center or right alignment, top, middle or bottom position and a text color. The same settings are in the inspector for one or many selected shapes. The shape grows taller as you type instead of hiding the text behind a scrollbar. Formatting is stored as a small markup in the text (`**bold**`, `- item`), so older diagrams, exports and AI apps keep working; pasted text arrives plain.
- **Image size for block images.** A block's image used to show at an automatic size. The editor now has an *Image size* slider (20 to 100 percent of the screen width) with a reset, and the preview and the participant view follow it. Test designs (first click, five second, preference, prototypes) keep their full size so results stay comparable.
- **Dropdown question.** A new *Dropdown* block in Questions picks one answer from a list that stays closed, for long lists such as countries or departments. Options can be pasted as a list, the text shown before choosing is editable, and an optional "Other" asks for the text. It uses the phone's own picker, works with logic (is / is not), and its results, CSV and MCP reference work like single-choice multiple choice.
- **Architecture section.** A new *Architecture* tab in every project holds diagrams and sitemaps. Diagrams are a FigJam-style canvas (React Flow, MIT): 13 shapes including decision, database, page and page group for information architecture, sticky notes, text and sections that carry what they contain; arrows drawn from any side of a shape or dropped anywhere on another one, with solid or dashed lines, arrowheads at one end, both or none, elbow, curved or straight paths and labels; colors, text sizes, undo/redo, copy/paste/duplicate, keyboard shortcuts, minimap and PNG/SVG export. Sitemaps are an indented outline drawn as a tree while you type, and can be opened as an editable diagram. Five starters (blank, sitemap, user flow, flowchart, system architecture) in Spanish or English. Both are stored as documents (kinds `diagram` and `sitemap`, no migration), so autosave, comments, *Save as…*, duplicate and read-only share links work as for any document; they are listed in Architecture instead of *Documents*. The MCP server gets a `diagram_reference` tool, and a test keeps its list of shapes equal to the canvas's.
- **Time study is now an observation log.** The *Time study* method is a fillable document modelled on a real study kept in a spreadsheet, instead of a timed-task survey. An observer logs one row per activity (person, date, start and end, activity bucket, main tool, other tools, matter or reference, notes); duration computes itself and a new row starts where the last one ended. A Summary tab gives, per person and in total, days observed, hours logged, average hours per day, hours with the measured tools as main tool and their weekly estimate, then hours, share of logged time and estimated hours per week by activity and by main tool, with a line for rows that have no bucket or tool instead of dropping them. A Schedule tab plans who is observed on which day, and a Buckets and tools tab holds the people, editable activity buckets with what counts in each, the tools (marking the ones being measured) and the workdays per week. It starts with generic buckets and tools, shows example rows only as a guide, and exports the summary, log, schedule and lists to Word, PDF, Markdown and CSV. Studies already built from the old template keep working, and the timed-task block is still available in any study.
- **MCP server for AI apps.** `/mcp` lets Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor and other MCP clients work in your projects as you: 18 tools to list and read projects, studies, results, insights and documents, create and edit them, and launch, close or publish studies. Nothing can be deleted, project roles apply, and results leave out participant details. Sign in with OAuth 2.1 (dynamic client registration, PKCE, consent screen at `/authorize`, rotating refresh tokens) or use a personal access token from *My account → Outside access (MCP)*, where connected apps and tokens are listed and revoked (migration 015).
- **Voice answers.** Participants can dictate open questions and the preference "why" follow-up instead of typing: the browser's speech recognition transcribes into the same field, in the study language, and the text stays editable. No audio is recorded or stored. Hidden where the browser has no speech recognition; a per-study toggle (Design → Allow voice answers, on by default) turns it off.
- **Welcome wizard.** New accounts get a four-step first run (migration 013): profile photo, name and language; workspace (just me, or create an organization); first project; first study from one of four recommended methods. Every step can be skipped and "Finish later" leaves at any point; people invited to an organization or a project skip the steps they don't need. The flag is stored on the account, so the wizard never shows twice, on any device.
- **Getting started and Help.** The Home shows a five-step checklist (project, study, publish, first response, shared report) checked off from real data, until it is complete or hidden. A new Help page in the account menu has short how-tos for the main tasks and a glossary of the concepts the interface uses (project, study, method, block, participant, response, insight, report, document, draft vs. published, prototype, organization, roles, comments). The wizard can be replayed from there.
- **Organizations.** Anyone can create an organization (a personal studio or a company) with a logo and a color (migration 012). Projects can live in an organization: every member sees them, admins and owners manage them, and new studies inherit the organization's brand; public reports show its logo. People are added by email or invited with a 7-day link that joins the organization on sign-up. Deleting an organization turns its projects back into personal projects.
- **Commenter role and comments.** A new project role between editor and read-only: commenters see everything and leave comments on the project, on a study's results and on a document. Organization members are commenters on every organization project. Authors and the project owner delete comments.
- **Time study.** New research method and block: a timed task the participant does in the live product (a link that opens in a new tab) or off-screen, with a stopwatch that can be hidden, an optional time limit and a reference time. Results show success rate, median, mean and range, the central half or the delta against the reference, how many gave up or ran out of time, and one bar per participant sorted by time with the median and reference marked. The template chains three timed tasks with a SEQ scale after each; pass/fail logic works like in prototype tasks; the CSV lists outcome and seconds.
- **Sign in with Google and Microsoft.** Optional OpenID Connect login (authorization code with PKCE, state and nonce; ID token verified against the provider's keys; migration 014). Buttons appear on the login and invitation pages when a provider is configured in `.env`. The provider's stable subject is stored per user; a verified email that matches an existing account links to it, otherwise an account is created under the same rules as the form (first account is admin, closed registration honors invitations). *My account* lists linked accounts to link or unlink them, lets provider-only accounts create a password, and never removes the last way to sign in. Password-less accounts confirm deletion by typing their email.

- **Project reports.** A shared report can combine several studies of a project (migration 005).
